## The task

Content research is more than collecting links. The goal is to identify what the audience needs, separate verified facts from assumptions, organize source notes, and hand the writer a structure that reduces rework.

## Research structure

| Section | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Audience | Who the content is for and what they are trying to accomplish. |
| Core question | The one problem the piece should resolve. |
| Source notes | Primary or credible references with short factual takeaways. |
| Questions to answer | Subtopics that deserve a clear response. |
| Outline | A logical H1/H2/H3 structure based on the reader journey. |
| Open items | Claims, numbers, product details, or examples that still need confirmation. |

## Working method

1. Clarify audience, purpose, format, and desired action.
2. Search for reliable sources and keep the original links.
3. Summarize facts in original wording rather than copying source prose.
4. Group recurring audience questions into a clear structure.
5. Flag anything uncertain or time-sensitive.
6. Create a concise outline and handoff notes.
7. Review for duplication, unsupported claims, and missing context.

## Why this helps

A writer can start from a structured brief instead of an empty page. An editor can see which claims need confirmation. The source trail remains available for review rather than disappearing inside a long document.
